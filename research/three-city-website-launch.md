# Website launch: Indore, Ujjain and Dewas

## Confirmed by the founder

- Launch cities: Indore, Ujjain and Dewas, Madhya Pradesh.
- Product channel: website only, usable from mobile and desktop browsers.
- No custom Android/iOS customer or staff app in current scope.

Smartphone-first catalogue, buy/sell services and a shared hub remain recommendations from the research, rather than newly confirmed founder decisions. Budgets, launch date, existing team, premises and suppliers have not been provided.

## Proposed website structure

| Page or area | Launch behaviour |
|---|---|
| Home | Clear Sell Phone and Buy Refurbished entry points; named three-city coverage |
| City/serviceability | City selector plus PIN-code check; changing city revalidates slots and delivery |
| Sell phone | Brand/model/variant, condition questions, estimate, inspection terms and booking |
| Buy phones | Available inventory, filters, grade, battery disclosure, photos and warranty |
| Product page | Physical-unit evidence, included accessories, total payable amount and delivery check |
| Booking/checkout | Address, contact verification, available slot, consent and payment where applicable |
| Customer account | Orders, pickup status, receipts, returns and support requests |
| Trust and policies | Grading guide, inspection process, returns, warranty, privacy and grievance contact |
| Support | Contact form and click-to-contact links; staff handles actual support |
| Staff portal | Assigned pickups, inspection photos/checklists, quote approval and custody records |
| Admin portal | Prices, stock, orders, payments, claims, roles and city-specific capacity |

Use a single responsive website and shared backend. Keep staff routes permission-protected. Customers should never need to install an app to book, buy, track or raise a claim. Messaging is an optional notification channel; the website remains the source of order status.

## Geographic setup

Create service-area records for Indore, Ujjain and Dewas with validated PIN codes. Do not infer supported coverage from a city name alone. For each area configure pickup days, capacity, cut-off, delivery estimate, charges and reverse-logistics availability. Unsupported addresses should receive a clear message before checkout or booking confirmation.

Proposed route model: one shared refurbishment/stock hub, with Indore as the initial candidate and scheduled collections for Ujjain and Dewas. This is a hypothesis requiring local costs and available facilities. A store in every city is not a technical requirement. If local partners collect devices, record each custody transfer and the time/cost to reach the lab.

Offer all three cities in the website from launch, but display only real slots. Do not promise same-day collection/delivery until staffing, route testing and inventory support it. Give customers a rescheduling option and track missed collections by city.

## Website-only inspection limits

Agent phone browsers can capture checklists and photographs where browser/device permissions permit. They cannot be assumed to read IMEI, reliably assess battery health or run every hardware diagnostic. Use manual verification and lab testing; record the method and timestamp. Background GPS tracking and offline synchronisation are not launch commitments. A failed upload must not silently mark inspection evidence as complete.

## Sensible delivery slices

1. Launch foundation: city/PIN eligibility, catalogue, sell booking, support, policies and secure admin.
2. Transaction readiness: inspection evidence, approved quotes, payout reconciliation and serialised stock.
3. Retail readiness: real product inventory, payment verification, delivery, returns and warranty handling.
4. Improvements after pilot: customer-visible device reports, route planning, better search and pricing analytics.

A lead-generation website can launch earlier than full transactional retail, but should not advertise purchase/fulfilment capabilities that are not operational. Automated pricing, custom payment ledgers and multi-role tooling account for much of the fuller platform effort even without apps.

## Financial treatment

The existing `unit-economics.csv` contains illustrative per-device scenarios, not city quotations. Keep one shared inventory/overhead model and add city/channel reporting. Do not multiply the previous inventory example or technology budget by three.

Allocate actual route cost using completed pickups and transfers, including unsuccessful visits. Track city-specific marketing spend, completed acquisitions, net retained sales, repair cost, returns and contribution. A shared hub can reduce duplicated fixed costs but adds transfer time/cost.

Two added CSV sensitivities increase combined per-unit logistics from ₹450 to ₹650 and ₹850. They model possible additional transport expense across the cluster; neither is assigned to a particular city or represented as a vendor rate. With all other base assumptions unchanged, contribution becomes ₹1,350 and ₹1,150 respectively.

The research report's ₹25–50 lakh custom-MVP estimate already described a web build with staff workflows. It is an illustrative staffed delivery model, not a required budget. The ₹3–8 lakh basic/manual stage has narrower scope. Actual cost should be recomputed from the chosen launch slice, available team and local operations. The later native-app expansion estimate has been removed from the current plan.

## Launch checks

- Each supported city has tested addresses, real capacity and a returns route.
- Unsupported PIN codes cannot place an unfulfillable order.
- Mobile-browser booking, file upload, checkout and tracking work without app installation.
- One inventory unit cannot be sold twice across different cities.
- A duplicate payment callback cannot create duplicate fulfilment or payout.
- Every price reduction has evidence and customer acceptance.
- Staff access is restricted by role and assignment; public pages do not expose full IMEI or customer details.
- City-level contribution and shared cash requirements can be reconciled.

These are implementation requirements, not claims that a website has already been built or tested.
